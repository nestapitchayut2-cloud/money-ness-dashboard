## ✍️ WRITING TASK 1 (Academic) — 20 นาที จับเวลา

**The chart below shows the percentage breakdown of revenue sources for two types of retail forex brokers, described as "A-Book" and "B-Book", in a typical year.**

**Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.**

| Revenue source | A-Book Broker (%) | B-Book Broker (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Spread markup | 50 | 20 |
| Commission | 30 | 5 |
| Swap / rollover fees | 20 | 10 |
| Client trading losses | 0 | 65 |

⚠️ **ตัวเลขเป็นข้อมูลสมมติสร้างขึ้นเพื่อฝึกเขียนโดยเฉพาะ** (ไม่ใช่รายงานจริงของโบรกเกอร์รายใดรายหนึ่ง) — สัดส่วนออกแบบให้สะท้อนกลไกจริงที่บทความ Reading เมื่อวาน (A-Book vs B-Book) อธิบายไว้: A-Book ไม่มีรายได้จากการขาดทุนของลูกค้าเลยเพราะส่งออเดอร์ตรงไปตลาด ส่วน B-Book กำไรหลักมาจากการเป็นคู่สัญญากับลูกค้าเอง

## 🔑 Model Answer (ห้ามเลื่อนมาดูก่อนทำเสร็จ!)

**Model answer (~205 คำ) — เทียบหลังเขียนเสร็จเท่านั้น**

The bar chart compares how two types of retail forex brokers, referred to as "A-Book" and "B-Book", generate revenue, across four categories: spread markup, commission, swap or rollover fees, and profits from client trading losses.

Overall, the two models are structured very differently. A-Book brokers earn all of their income from fee-based sources and report no revenue from client losses, whereas B-Book brokers rely on client losses as their single largest income stream.

For A-Book brokers, spread markup is the leading source, making up exactly half of total revenue at 50%, followed by commission at 30% and swap fees at a further 20%; together, these three channels account for the entirety of their income. Client trading losses contribute nothing at all, since A-Book brokers pass orders straight to the market and hedge their exposure.

By contrast, client trading losses represent almost two-thirds of B-Book revenue, at 65%, considerably outweighing the other three categories combined. Spread markup accounts for just 20% here, while commission, at only 5%, and swap fees, at 10%, play a comparatively minor role.

In short, A-Book brokers spread their earnings fairly evenly across three service-based fees, whereas B-Book brokers concentrate the bulk of their income in a single, riskier source: their own clients' losses.
